Since you haven’t picked yet, my default would be Oyado Koto no Yume. At about $260 a night, two nights is roughly $320 less than Hiranoya Kachoan. It’s also closer to the old town, so you can walk to the morning markets and the Sanmachi Suji streets before the day-trippers arrive. And the saved money covers the Shirakawa-go day trip.

Hiranoya Kachoan makes more sense if the private onsen and a splurge night are what you’re looking forward to most. In that case, I’d put the extra $320 toward that and skip the Shirakawa-go detour.

Two practical tips for either choice:

Book now. Takayama’s spring festival (Sanno Matsuri) is around April 14 to 15, and cherry blossom season fills up small ryokan well in advance. If your dates are close to that, book as soon as you can.
Mention vegetarian when you reserve, not at check-in. Kaiseki menus are set days ahead, and you’ll want them to plan for strict vegetarian, including dashi, which is often fish-based.

You could also take the direct bus from Kanazawa to Takayama, which stops at Shirakawa-go on the way. That gives you a look at the village without a separate day trip, though the timing and luggage handling are worth checking.
